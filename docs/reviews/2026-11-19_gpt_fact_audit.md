## 1. Numerical errors and evidence mismatches

1. CRITICAL — pp. 4, 6: InfiniSST is not a controlled baseline. Table 1 mixes the historical RASST no-retrieval results with new ten-talk runs. InfiniSST's 61.4% TERM_ACC is the unweighted mean of 76.55% ACL and 46.28% medicine; the other systems use pooled accuracy over 2,047 occurrences. Its BLEU and MT-BLEU also use two corpus-level scores rather than the new pooled scoring protocol. Therefore, p. 6's claim that retrieval improves TERM_ACC by approximately 27 points is not a valid like-for-like estimate. The arithmetic is correct; the comparison is not. Sources: autoterm_1m_budget_search_20260711.md, README.md, autoterm_10talk_streamlaal_20260711.tsv.

2. p. 6: “within 0.8 TERM_ACC points” is numerically false at full precision. Run 2 AutoTerm − Merged-10k is −0.83048 points. Write “approximately 0.8 points” or “within 0.9 points.” More importantly, Merged-10k has higher observed TERM_ACC: 88.13% versus 87.30%. Sources: taurus/paired_significance.json, taurus_runs.md.

3. pp. 1, 2, 6: the second run is not independent dataset replication. Both runs reuse the same ten talks, glossary-derived benchmark terminology, and evaluation configuration. Run 2 changes hardware and execution conditions. AutoTerm TERM_ACC moves from 88.32% to 87.30%, while its difference from Known-domain changes sign (+0.24 to −0.68 points). “Independent run” is defensible only as a separate execution, not independent evidence of generalization. Sources: taurus_runs.md, paired_significance.json, taurus/paired_significance.json.

4. p. 9, Table 4: generated-prototype median Top-1 is misrounded. The ten Top-1 values in router_replay_generated.json have middle values 70.62% and 70.86%; their median is 70.74%, which rounds to 70.7%, not 70.8%. The 85.8% Top-4 median is correct. This is a minor numerical error, not a substantive result change.

5. pp. 4–9: the attachment set does not permit independent recomputation of the principal results. paired_significance.json and taurus/paired_significance.json contain final statistics, not per-talk metric inputs or BLEU sufficient statistics. evidence.zip lacks the referenced runs/*.json, scores/*/segments.jsonl, gold alignments, reference translations, original glossary manifests, full scoring scripts, and full July run configuration. README.md explicitly says full runs and outputs were pending release; taurus_runs.md says the July configuration was reconstructed because the complete original was not retained. Traceable to a derived JSON is not independently reproducible from the attachments.

6. p. 6: the latency numbers omit measurement conditions. The reported 2.3 s first translation, 0.57 s p50, and 1.17 s p95 are correctly copied from g1_serving_summary.json, specifically vllm_serve_bge_gpu, one session, two A6000s. But the test uses 180 seconds of audio, excludes the first three chunks from chunk-latency statistics, and discards a cold-start warm-up (approximately 13 s to first text). “Each chunk returns its text” is stronger than the measured post-start-up quantiles. Sources: taurus_runs.md, g1_serving_summary.json.

7. pp. 6, 8: the 85.0% → 97.0% pinning comparison is not an isolated ablation. pin_counterfactual.json reuses the live run's already-confirmed slice, then compares raw Top-4 selection with confirmed-slice-plus-Top-3. Confirmation depends on other routing rules; translation outputs remain fixed. The replay also scores 8,775 windows versus 8,776 in router_replay.json. The coverage figures are supported, but the causal assertion that pinning accounts for most of the gain is not established. Source: README.md, pinning-counterfactual section.

8. pp. 8–9, Appendix B: identical router configuration is incompletely auditable. taurus_runs.md states that the complete July configuration was not retained and that October parameters were reconstructed from scripts and notes. README.md also states that the original 1k manifest was unavailable for the router replay; its prototype descriptions were reconstructed using a 10k manifest. The files support the intended configuration, not exact historical configuration identity.

9. p. 6: the 203 ms “routing and retrieval” time includes more than routing and retrieval. The recorded retrieve_s stage includes shared speech encoding, BGE-M3 context similarity, router operations, and active-index queries. The figure is supported as a composite stage time, not as the incremental cost of AutoTerm's router. Source: autoterm_1m_budget_search_20260711.md, “System compute scaling.”

10. pp. 3–5: several implementation and availability claims lack attached verification artifacts. These include background index preloading, session-consistent snapshots during hot registration, registration without restarting the service, the MIT license, one-command launcher, hosted entry page, and screencast. Figure 2 shows a recorded-output replay, not proof of these operational behaviors. evidence.zip contains no implementation source, deployment manifest, integration-test results, or screencast. Treat these as unverified by the attachments, not disproved.

## 2. Statistical and confounding vulnerabilities

11. HIGH — Non-significance is repeatedly presented as parity. Run 1 AutoTerm − Known-domain TERM_ACC has 95% CI [−0.95, +1.49] points; Run 2 has [−1.48, 0.00]. Losses approaching 1.5 points are compatible with the reported intervals. No noninferiority margin, equivalence test, or independent holdout exists. Sources: paired_significance.json, taurus/paired_significance.json.

12. HIGH — Run 2 is not compute-condition matched. taurus_runs.md records AutoTerm as a single session on its own two-GPU service (sst-a), while Known-domain, Merged-2k, and Merged-10k run as three concurrent sessions on another two-GPU service (sst-b). It also records wall-clock-based router warm-up, update, and cooldown behavior. Equal audio chunks do not imply equivalent GPU load, generation scheduling, or routing dynamics. The p. 2 and p. 5 assertion that compared policies differ only in searchable terminology indexes is too strong.

13. HIGH — The same-catalog no-routing baseline already eliminates demonstrated routing necessity at 10k. Run 2 Merged-10k searches all 9,880 distinct mappings without user domain input and gets 88.13% TERM_ACC versus AutoTerm's 87.30%, with BLEU 44.97 versus 45.15. The 0.83-point TERM_ACC advantage is not significant by the talk permutation test (p = 0.084), but it is directionally against AutoTerm. Only two domains occur, and a four-slice router has enough slots to retain both relevant slices. Sources: taurus_runs.md, taurus/paired_significance.json, taurus/prompt_precision.json.

14. HIGH — The main statistical result has an unresolved multiplicity choice. AutoTerm − Merged-1M is correctly reported at +3.908 TERM_ACC points (p = 0.01171875) and +2.300 BLEU (p = 0.001953125). But README.md explicitly reports Holm-adjusted TERM_ACC p = 0.082 when its 12 comparisons are treated as one family; BLEU and MT-BLEU remain at p = 0.023. Restricting the family to the three AutoTerm-versus-1M metrics preserves significance, but no primary endpoint was preregistered. “Both significant” means nominally significant under the selected comparison family, not robust to every reasonable multiplicity definition.

15. HIGH — Run 2's bootstrap CI and permutation test disagree on significance. For AutoTerm − Merged-10k TERM_ACC, the 95% bootstrap CI is [−1.58, −0.25], excluding zero, but the permutation p-value is 0.084. This is not necessarily an arithmetic error: percentile bootstrap intervals need not invert permutation tests. It is an unexplained inferential inconsistency. The same pattern occurs in the unpublished Run 2 term-free comparison against Known-domain: CI [+0.021, +2.008] but permutation p = 0.215. State which inference is primary and explain the discrepancy. Sources: taurus/paired_significance.json, taurus/term_free_quality.json.

16. HIGH — The test data influenced router configuration. Appendix B admits thresholds were chosen on four windows drawn from the evaluation talks. README.md confirms no new talks, different playback orders, or independent decoding seeds were used for the principal evaluation. The 4,000 stratified bootstrap resamples do not fix selection on test data. Freeze settings and test on untouched talks. Sources: README.md, taurus_runs.md.

17. HIGH — Exact enumeration does not make the statistical assumptions exact. Enumerating all 2^10 = 1,024 talk-level swaps is correctly implemented at the summary level. But the ten talks belong to one continuous adaptive session; routing state, confirmed slice, and translation context can carry across boundaries. Talk-level output exchangeability is therefore not guaranteed. With five talks per corpus, the bootstrap CIs also depend heavily on a small number of clusters. The p-values quantify the selected conditional test, not generalization over future streams. Sources: paired_significance.json, README.md, Appendix A.

18. HIGH — The translation-quality evidence is disproportionately medical. Of the 935 reference-defined term-free sentences, 888 are medicine and only 47 are ACL. The +2.19 BLEU AutoTerm-versus-1M result is principally a medicine result: +2.23 BLEU medicine versus +0.19 ACL. ESO references are model-generated, and decisions.md documents an unresolved GPT-5.4-versus-Gemini provenance conflict. Human-verified ESO terminology does not establish human-verified Chinese sentence references. Sources: term_free_quality.json, decisions.md, README.md.

19. HIGH — Benchmark construction favors the routed system's intended operating regime. The correct NLP and medicine slices already contain evaluation terminology. The experiment evaluates retrieval competition and routing among known entries, not discovery of unseen technical terminology or unseen domains. All ten evaluated talks fall into two relevant domains. A static two-slice selection already guarantees correct-slice inclusion by construction. Sources: autoterm_1m_budget_search_20260711.md, README.md, taurus_runs.md.

20. MEDIUM — The catalog-size curve combines experiments with different hardware and catalog construction. Merged-2k/10k run on two A6000 GPUs; Merged-100k/1M run on an H200. Merged-10k is constructed by deduplicating ten 1k slice prefixes; Merged-100k is a prefix of the 1M catalog. The evidence does not establish that all four conditions form one consistently nested size sweep under identical execution. The 100k-to-1M comparison is controlled within Run 1; a universal 10k-to-100k degradation threshold is not. Sources: taurus_runs.md, autoterm_1m_budget_search_20260711.md.

21. MEDIUM — Prompt noise is an interpretation, not an isolated causal effect. The approximate number of time-local gold-matching injected references per chunk, computed as Refs/chunk × prompt precision, is:

| Run 1 policy | Mean injected pairs | Mean gold-matching pairs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AutoTerm | 0.944 | 0.328 |
| Merged-100k | 4.955 | 0.339 |
| Merged-1M | 8.700 | 0.300 |

Merged-100k does not have fewer gold-matching references per chunk than AutoTerm under this proxy, despite losing 1.37 BLEU. Extra injected material is associated with worse BLEU, but these observations do not isolate displacement, conflicting translations, or model sensitivity to unnecessary prompt text. The 935-sentence analysis likewise cannot establish causality; “no glossary string in the reference” does not mean “no technical terminology.” Sources: autoterm_10talk_streamlaal_20260711.tsv, term_free_quality.json, README.md.

22. p. 6: the strongest defensible result is narrower than the conclusion. The Run 1 arithmetic checks out: AutoTerm 1,808/2,047 versus Merged-1M 1,728/2,047 yields +3.908 points; BLEU 45.090898 versus 42.791173 yields +2.299725. The H200 RTF of 0.405894 corresponds to 2.464× real-time throughput. These are valid reported measurements within their protocols, not independent demonstrations of portability or equivalence. Source: autoterm_10talk_streamlaal_20260711.tsv, autoterm_1m_budget_search_20260711.md.

23. p. 6: the 96.9% working-set coverage hides boundary-specific failures. README.md reports 70 of 2,047 term occurrences in windows where the correct slice is absent; AutoTerm gets 53/70 correct versus Known-domain's 58/70. This is descriptive, not causal, but it shows that global window coverage cannot substitute for boundary-conditioned terminology accuracy. Source: README.md, “缺口窗口的术语.”

24. p. 6: “translation around terms” is not cleanly isolated by MT-BLEU. Masking recognized target strings does not necessarily remove incorrect translated variants, paraphrases, or downstream changes induced by the term prompt. A positive MT-BLEU difference supports a broader output difference after masking; it does not prove the remaining translation improved independently of terminology errors. Sources: README.md, autoterm_1m_budget_search_20260711.md.

25. p. 6: the latency conclusion must remain single-session-specific. The 180-second G1 single-session measurement supports real-time operation under its stated configuration. It does not establish sustained real-time behavior at arbitrary concurrency. Under the same GPU-hosted BGE-M3 serving configuration, 8 sessions have p95 1.74 s, while 16 have p95 5.17 s and 32 have p95 16.86 s, against 1.92-second chunks. decisions.md correctly removes the old 32-session claim. Sources: g1_serving_summary.json, taurus_runs.md, decisions.md.

26. p. 6: deletion of limitations makes the remaining general claims harder to defend. decisions.md explicitly records removal of the Limitations section because it consumes main-text space. Restore a compact limitations statement covering benchmark-seeded terminology, ten reused talks, two domains, open-loop routing diagnostics, generated medical references, lack of equivalence testing, and single-session latency. The evaluation's Data paragraph currently discloses only one of these limitations.
