\section{Statistical comparisons}
\label{app:stats}

Table~\ref{tab:stats} reports all paired comparisons. The exact permutation test
swaps outputs between the two systems within each talk and recomputes the pooled
metric for all 1,024 combinations. The smallest attainable two-sided $p$-value is
0.002. Significance
claims rest on this test. With ten talks, the percentile bootstrap can disagree
with it near the boundary: for the MT-BLEU difference from similarity
routing, the interval excludes zero while $p=0.13$. Confidence
intervals come from 4,000 stratified bootstrap resamples of five ACL and five
ESO talks. Removing any single talk preserves all three advantages of
AutoTerm-SST over Merged-1M, with gains of at least 2.57 TERM\_ACC points, 2.04
BLEU, and 1.70 MT-BLEU points.

\section{Router configuration}
\label{app:config}

The language-model router adds a single parameter to the shared settings of
Table~\ref{tab:config}, the switch rate $\rho$, against five for the
similarity router. The similarity router uses BGE-M3~\citep{chen-etal-2024-m3} to
embed the last 128 tokens of $c_t$ and each prototype with its Chinese translation. It
pins the confirmed slice in the working set. A switch requires a candidate to lead
both the runner-up and the confirmed slice by fixed margins for three consecutive
updates outside a 30-second cooldown. We selected these thresholds on four short
segments drawn from the evaluation talks before running the complete streams.
Because both routers update on wall-clock time, we ran each router alone on its own server.

\section{Router diagnostics}
\label{app:router}

Open-loop replays feed both deployed routers the same saved ACL-ESO stream
translations, using the context defined in Section~\ref{sec:adaptive-memory}. As in
the live system, scores refresh at most once every 5 seconds of audio. On each
refresh, the similarity router applies its confirmation rule and pin, and the
language-model router takes one filter step. The similarity router reproduces its 19
live topic switches when replay uses the live emission times.

\paragraph{Short topic shifts.}
The posterior follows topics that alternate every 15 to 300 seconds. For each
length in Table~\ref{tab:router-diag}, we cut the saved translations of the NLP
and medicine talks into segments of that length and interleave them into one
40-segment stream. At every segment length, at least half the segments start with the
incoming topic slice in the language-model router's working set. The slice ranks
first after a median of 7.7 seconds. With its confirmation rule, the similarity
router takes a median of 27 to 46 seconds to confirm the incoming topic slice in
segments of one minute or longer.

\paragraph{Other target languages.}
Routing coverage carries over to German and Japanese. We replay RASST's translations
of the same talks in each language. For each replay, we change only the language
named in the router prompt.

\paragraph{Close domains.}
Finer slices separate the two routers further. The same 4B model splits the NLP and
medicine catalogs into eight subtopics each. It proposes subtopics from 400 terms
outside the evaluation glossaries and assigns each term by letter probability. The
catalog also retains the eight other topics. Table~\ref{tab:router-diag}
reports coverage over the 2,047 gold term occurrences by tracking their topic slices
in the working set. In hindsight, choosing the best four slices separately for
each talk yields coverage of 97.3\% of the gold term occurrences.

\paragraph{Generated prototypes and late updates.}
An LLM can write the prototypes. GPT-5.5 wrote one prototype per topic slice from its
glossary after removing every evaluation term. Replacing hand-written prototypes
with generated ones lowers the language-model router's top-four coverage to 97.9\%
with per-chunk updates on the ACL-ESO stream. The RealSI streams use
generated prototypes because the hand-written NLP prototype names topics from the
RealSI talk mapped to NLP. Closed-loop runs in Section~\ref{sec:evaluation} scored
each update before the next chunk. When audio arrives faster than real time, the
deployed worker in Section~\ref{sec:adaptive-memory} can apply an update one chunk
late. That delay lowers coverage in Table~\ref{tab:router-diag} by at most 0.33
points.
